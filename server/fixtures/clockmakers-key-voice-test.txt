The Clockmaker's Key

The bell above the shop door rang just as every clock in the room stopped.

Old Agnes Whitlow looked up from her workbench, her spectacles sliding down her nose. "Well," she murmured, "that has never happened before."

A small girl stood in the doorway, holding a brass key almost as long as her arm. "Are you the clockmaker?" Pip asked breathlessly. "Grandpa said you'd know what this opens."

Before Agnes could reply, the door banged open again. A broad man in a dripping coat shoved past the girl. "There you are!" Inspector Hale barked. "That key is evidence, young lady. Hand it over. Now."

Pip clutched it tighter. "It's mine! Grandpa left it to me!"

Behind the inspector, a thin young man hovered on the step, wringing his cap. "Sir, please," Felix stammered. "She's only a child. Maybe we could, um, ask nicely?"

"Nicely?" Hale snorted. "Half the clocks in this city stopped tonight, Felix. I am done being nice."

Agnes rose slowly and crossed the room. She studied the key for a long moment, then smiled. "The clocks didn't stop, Inspector. They're waiting."

"Waiting for what?" Hale demanded.

A soft voice answered from the shadows at the back of the shop. Everyone turned. A tall woman in a grey cloak stepped forward, calm as still water.

"For the key to come home," said Lady Morrow. "I've been looking for it for forty years."

Pip's eyes went wide. "You knew my grandpa?"

"I did," Lady Morrow said gently. "He wound the great clock in the tower. Tonight, someone must wind it again."

Felix gulped. "The tower? In this storm?"

Agnes chuckled and reached for her coat. "Come along, then. Time won't wait forever."

Pip lifted the key, grinning, and somewhere above them, very faintly, a single clock began to tick.
